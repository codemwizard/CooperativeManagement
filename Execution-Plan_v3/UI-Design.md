I understand the opportunity much more clearly now. The UI problem and the commercial opportunity are connected: Symphony should feel like a guided transformation of an enterprise, while quietly building the persistent data infrastructure that makes the enterprise useful to multiple institutions over time.

The prototype must make two things visible at once:

1. The immediate transformation: take a poorly documented cooperative and make its existing economic reality legible, organised and ready for institutional assessment without pretending its underlying business has magically improved.

2. The accumulating value: every cooperative, member, project, claim, evidence item, verification and subsequent update contributes to a reusable record that can serve NABII, SBGI, lenders, investors, TA providers and portfolio managers.

Your Lima Links reference supports the second opportunity. Its project document describes growth from 37,000 registered farmer users in 2018 to over 175,000, with a substantial active user base. More importantly, the model connects farmer registration to market access, supplier relationships, finance, climate information and ongoing data collection. It describes using farmer and transaction data to help develop credit scoring and agricultural finance products.

The lesson is not that Symphony should become Lima Links. It is that onboarding can be the beginning of an enduring institutional relationship rather than a one-off registration exercise.

There is an important distinction: Lima Links demonstrates a potential distribution and data-collection model, not proof that cooperatives will consistently maintain Symphony records or that institutions will pay for them. Those are hypotheses we should design the journeys to test.

Illustrative wireframe only. The middle column contains the current task, brief context and progression controls. No permanent sidebar or collection of competing menus.

I would apply these rules throughout:

* One primary task per screen. Avoid presenting governance, evidence, readiness and analytics all at once.

* One dominant Continue button. Back and Save for later are secondary. Show other actions only when relevant.

* Progression, not tab-hopping. Use a linear sequence with sensible branches when a discrepancy or missing requirement needs attention.

* Context without clutter. A short explanation can describe what this step accomplishes and why it matters, without turning every contextual element into a button.

* Persistent state underneath. Moving forward changes the workflow stage; it must not create a new, disconnected record.

* Visible completion and exceptions. At each stage, show what is done, what is missing and what happens next.

The outer columns should remain empty as you requested. Don't fill them with decorative cards, activity feeds or help panels just because the space is available.

# 2. SME journey — from a weak paper pack to Assessment Ready

This should be the flagship journey for the NABII demonstration.

The starting SME should be deliberately unattractive on paper. Use a fictional cooperative with an expired cooperative certificate, outdated ZRA compliance, an incomplete transaction history, missing governance records, and office holders who no longer match the certificate.

But the transformation must be honest.

Symphony improves the organisation, traceability and assessability of the information. It does not improve the cooperative's underlying finances by changing how the information is presented.

## The guided sequence

01 · ACCOUNT

Login → Register

The user creates an account or is assisted by a workshop operator. Keep the choice of role and access scope simple.

02 · IDENTITY

Register the enterprise

Enter the name, location, contact details, registration type and available registration references. Save the record even if some documents are missing.

03 · COMPLIANCE

Record regulatory status

Check the available PACRA or cooperative registration information and ZRA status. Record the source, date and result, distinguishing an expired document from a verified current status.

04 · GOVERNANCE

Establish who is authorised to act

Record the chairman, secretary and treasurer. Compare current entries with the available certificate or official record and flag discrepancies during data entry. Assign the cooperative verifier a separate role.

05 · OPERATIONS

Build the evidence timeline

Upload the records already available: transaction slips, off-taker agreements, FISP or SAFF records where applicable, invoices and production records. Expose gaps rather than inventing missing history.

06 · MEMBERSHIP

Register members and households

Enrol members under the cooperative, then drill down into an individual member's household and farming profile where there is a legitimate purpose and appropriate consent.

07 · BASELINE

Record basic ESG and project indicators

Capture the baseline information available now, identify its source and allow later updates. Don't make complete ESG data a prerequisite for creating the enterprise record.

08 · READINESS

Review the readiness record

Present the evidence assembled, unresolved gaps, governance discrepancies, verification status and applicable financing or programme destinations.

The sequence is a logical workflow, not eight mandatory visits for every enterprise. A user should be able to save and resume, skip unavailable evidence, and return to the appropriate step when assistance is needed.

## The workshop must be a first-class part of this journey

Your operational model is more specific than ordinary self-service onboarding:

1. Workshop day: assisted registration of enterprises using five laptops.

2. Following week: each cooperative registers members and uploads its existing, fragmented information at its own pace.

3. Targeted assistance: Symphony agents visit locations where help is needed.

4. Phone follow-up: routine clarification and reminders continue remotely.

5. Ongoing updates: the cooperative's designated people continue adding evidence and updating records.

The demonstration should make this operational sequence visible. It should not imply that a workshop completes every cooperative's governance and compliance remediation in one day.

Your 50-SMEs-per-day target is a testable throughput hypothesis. Measure registration time, assistance required, completion rate and the proportion that progress to a usable initial record. Don't present the target as proven until the workflow has been tested.

## Keep the service boundary explicit

Symphony's initial role is onboarding, evidence capture, provenance, straightforward administrative support and workflow coordination.

If the cooperative consents and the relevant TA agreement permits it, a separate service could help with straightforward online filing or compliance tasks. Specialist legal, tax, investment and other regulated advice remains the responsibility of appropriately qualified providers. The prototype should make that boundary visible rather than accidentally implying that Symphony itself certifies compliance.

# 4. NABII portal — from programme administration to a discoverable pipeline

NABII should enter a different progression. It doesn't need to experience all the SME's data-entry screens. It needs to see the aggregate effect of that work.

## The journey

1. Open the enterprise pipeline

A single screen shows the 200 SMEs in a selected programme or portfolio, distributed across four clearly defined readiness states.

2. Select a readiness state

Clicking a state opens the enterprises in it. The user can filter by district, sector, programme or the type of support required.

3. Diagnose the needs

The list reveals recurring gaps: governance, registration, financial records, project evidence, household/member data or independent verification.

4. Coordinate support

NABII identifies which enterprises need general assistance, which need specialist TA, and which are already suitable for assessment against a destination's requirements.

5. Discover the investable pipeline

NABII can see which enterprises are currently discoverable and assessment-ready for specific financing opportunities, then share or present those records to appropriate institutions.

# 5. SBGI / TA portal — identify where intervention changes readiness

SBGI needs to see where TA can unlock a usable financing pipeline, not another general-purpose SME directory.

Its progression should be:

1. View the supported pipeline. See enterprises by current state, location, sector and intended financing pathway.

2. Select a bottleneck. For example, incomplete financial records or governance discrepancies.

3. Inspect affected enterprises. Open the list and distinguish routine onboarding help from specialist intervention.

4. Recommend or assign TA. Record the intervention, responsible provider, scope and intended result.

5. Review the outcome. See which gaps were addressed, which remain unresolved and which require independent verification.

6. Identify candidates for PFI assessment. Present the updated evidence state without implying that SBGI or Symphony has approved a loan.

The key screen is the TA intervention queue. It should answer three questions: Who needs help? What kind? What changed after the help?

This also creates a way to test whether TA is effective. You can compare readiness before and after an intervention, while recognising that improved readiness does not by itself prove better repayment or lower credit losses.

# 6. PFI portal — discover, inspect, assess, decide

The PFI should not start at a blank application form. It should start with enterprises that may be worth considering.

The progression is:

Discover → Inspect → Query or verify → Assess → Decide → Monitor

## Screen 1: Find enterprises

A few filters:

* Sector or activity

* Location

* Financing purpose and range

* Green or impact criteria

* Readiness for the relevant financing destination

The result is a list of matching enterprises, each showing its current state, key evidence limitations and an action to inspect the record.

The PFI is not buying a score from Symphony. It is finding businesses it might otherwise never have considered.

## Screen 2: Inspect the enterprise

Keep the first view focused:

* Who is the enterprise?

* What is it doing?

* What is it seeking?

* What evidence supports its claims?

* What remains uncertain?

* What has been independently verified?

The PFI can progressively open the details. Don't dump the entire dossier on the first screen.

## Screen 3: Resolve a question

The PFI asks for a missing record or assigns a verification task. The request becomes part of the enterprise's record.

The SME supplies the evidence once. The appropriate parties can access the updated state according to their permissions.

## Screen 4: Record the decision

The PFI chooses to continue assessment, request more information, decline or approve through its own process.

Symphony records the outcome if authorised to do so. It does not make the lending decision.

## Screen 5: Continue after the decision

For a financed enterprise, the same record can support monitoring of milestones, governance changes, project evidence, exceptions and impact indicators.

For a declined enterprise, the record remains available for other appropriate opportunities, subject to the enterprise's consent and access controls.

This is the strongest commercial continuity story: one enterprise record can support multiple decisions and the lifecycle after financing.

# 7. VVB portal — verify a claim, not an entire enterprise

The VVB journey should be narrow and precise.

Assigned → Inspect → Verify → Record finding

1. Open assignment. Identify the claim and the scope of verification.

2. Inspect the basis. View the evidence, provenance and known limitations relevant to that claim.

3. Conduct verification. Record the method and evidence actually examined.

4. Record finding. Verified, partially verified, unable to verify or contradicted, as appropriate to the verification framework.

5. Publish the finding to the governed record. Authorised users can then see the result and its scope.

For example, a cooperative's member register may be supplied by its secretary and checked by a designated internal verifier. A separate VVB may be assigned to verify a project claim.

These are not the same assurance role. The internal cooperative verifier provides separation of duties and internal auditability; an independent VVB provides a distinct level of external assurance.

The UI must never imply that the presence of a verifier, or a recorded finding, certifies the entire enterprise.

# 8. Portfolio manager — monitor the portfolio as it changes

This deserves its own first-class journey, not a sub-tab hidden under the PFI.

The portfolio may belong to a bank, investor, NGO, donor, TA programme, guarantee programme or another organisation managing a defined group of enterprises.

Its journey:

Select portfolio → Review current state → Investigate exception → Assign intervention → Review change → Report outcome

The opening screen should answer:

* How many enterprises are in this portfolio?

* What state is each in?

* What has changed since the last review?

* Which exceptions need attention?

* Which milestones or impact claims need evidence?

* Which enterprises may need intervention?

Selecting an exception should take the user directly to the relevant enterprise, evidence or assignment—not to a maze of dashboard tabs.

The value here is not merely document storage. It is that the portfolio manager can see a governed history of what was claimed, what changed, what was verified, and what remains unresolved.

This is a natural extension of the architecture, although the commercial value still needs to be tested with actual portfolio managers.

# 9. The SME discovery marketplace — make continuity visible

I would design this as a discovery capability available to authorised institutions, rather than immediately building another standalone portal.

Its journey is:

1. Define the opportunity. An investor, lender or programme specifies the enterprises it wants to find.

2. Search the portfolio. Filter by sector, location, project, scale, evidence state and relevant requirements.

3. Inspect matches. Review why an enterprise appears relevant and what remains unknown.

4. Request access or engage. Obtain the appropriate permission to inspect more detailed information or initiate contact.

5. Reuse the existing record. Apply the new destination's requirements without forcing the SME to rebuild its profile.

This is where your example of a financially weak enterprise matters.

A business that fails to qualify for a bank loan may still have an advantageous location, a distribution network, access to customers, productive assets or strategic value to an investor.

Symphony should not hide weaknesses or label the enterprise universally “investable.” It should make the evidence and limitations discoverable so that different institutions can judge suitability for their own purposes.

One important constraint: discoverability does not mean unrestricted visibility. A cooperative's public profile, a lender's assessment dossier and a member's household details should have different access boundaries. Household income, children and other personal information should not be exposed to capital providers merely because the cooperative has registered its members.

# 10. The persistent data engine beneath every journey

Your statement that Symphony does not design projects, advise on compliance or business practice, or rely on AI is important to the product boundary.

I would translate it into one consistent pattern across the UI:

Claim → Source → Evidence → Governance → Current state → Permitted use

For example:

| Record                   | What Symphony should capture                                                        |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cooperative registration | Registration claim, supplied certificate, issuing source and recorded status        |
| Current office holders   | People, roles, effective dates and discrepancies with the available official record |
| Board decision           | Minutes, author, authorised submission role and supporting evidence                 |
| Member registration      | Member identity, relationship to the cooperative and authorised data scope          |
| Transaction              | Transaction claim, supporting record, date and any limitations                      |
| ESG baseline             | Indicator, value, measurement period, method and source                             |
| Independent finding      | Claim verified, scope, method, verifier and conclusion                              |

The key is that the system must not silently turn an assertion into a verified fact.

A person entering data can identify an obvious discrepancy through deterministic rules. No AI is required. A verification role can inspect supporting evidence. The resulting state should show what was supplied, what was checked and what remains unresolved.

This is also how the record becomes reusable: a subsequent institution can inspect the evidence and its limitations instead of relying on an unqualified statement copied into another application.

## The membership opportunity

Your membership-registration idea could become a substantial source of ongoing data, but the value proposition needs to be separated into three layers:

1. Cooperative management: maintaining member registers and updating relevant member or household information.

2. Institutional services: giving authorised organisations access to the particular information they need for financing, agricultural services, supply chains or impact programmes.

3. Analytics: producing properly scoped, aggregated or otherwise authorised insights from accumulated records.

The default verifier role is a useful governance mechanism, but it is not, by itself, an incentive to maintain the data. Cooperatives need a recurring reason to do so.

The most promising hypothesis is to tie updates to work that already happens: cooperative meetings, input distribution, seasonal planning, buyer transactions, TA visits and donor-supported activities. A short update becomes part of an existing workflow instead of an additional administrative burden.

The Lima Links example is relevant because its data collection was connected to tangible services—market information, buyer and supplier connections, financing and climate information—not simply to maintaining a database.

# 11. How to test the opportunity instead of just presenting it

I would define a small set of market tests alongside the UI design.

| Hypothesis                                               | Bellwether test                                                                                                    | What would weaken the thesis                                               |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Assisted onboarding can create records at scale          | Measure time per registration, assistance required and completion rates during a real workshop                     | Registration remains too labour-intensive to scale economically            |
| Cooperatives will maintain their records                 | Measure member updates and evidence additions after the initial workshop                                           | Records become stale once the programme ends                               |
| NABII needs a continuously visible pipeline              | Ask programme staff to use the cohort and readiness views to identify real interventions and candidate enterprises | They prefer their existing spreadsheets and see little operational benefit |
| TA can improve assessment readiness                      | Compare outstanding gaps before and after documented interventions                                                 | TA activity rarely resolves the gaps preventing assessment                 |
| PFIs will discover unfamiliar enterprises                | Give a PFI a relevant cohort and observe whether it inspects and accepts any enterprises for assessment            | It ignores the cohort or still requires the entire process to restart      |
| Evidence can be reused across institutions               | Track repeat use of existing evidence for a second destination                                                     | Institutions demand substantially duplicated records each time             |
| Portfolio managers need ongoing governed state           | Test whether a manager uses exceptions, milestones and verification findings in actual portfolio reviews           | The information adds little beyond existing monitoring systems             |
| A membership network can support recurring data services | Test whether a partner will fund an ongoing data-collection or update programme                                    | Partners want access but will not pay or provide sustainable incentives    |

These tests should produce observable results, not favourable opinions after a demonstration.

For the first workshop, I would measure at least:

* median time to create an initial enterprise record;

* number of enterprises registered per operator and per laptop;

* proportion of records with sufficient identity and contact information to continue;

* proportion that supply additional evidence within the following week;

* proportion needing an agent visit;

* time and cost to reach a defined destination-specific readiness state.

That will tell you whether the proposed five-laptop, 50-enterprise workshop is operationally plausible.

## The cooperative TAM needs a qualification

Your calculation is a useful scale scenario:

* 150,000 cooperatives as the assumed universe;

* 10% penetration = 15,000 cooperatives;

* 10 members each = 150,000 member records.

But this is not yet a validated total addressable market. The number of registered cooperatives, the number active, the number suitable for the initial service and the number willing to maintain digital records may differ substantially. Also, a cooperative with more than ten members could generate far more than ten member records, while inactive or dormant cooperatives may generate little usable value.

I would use the figures as a scenario for modelling network scale, not as a confirmed customer or revenue forecast.

# 12. The final UI design decision

I would now make the following changes to the prototype's design direction.

* Replace the persistent tab-heavy navigation with role-specific guided journeys.

* Keep the three-column layout, with empty outer columns and the active workflow centred.

* Use a small number of top-level destinations: My Enterprise, My Programme, Find Enterprises, My Assignments or My Portfolio, depending on role.

* Make the current step and overall progress visible without exposing every available function at once.

* Make Continue the primary action; reveal exception handling and specialist actions only when needed.

* End each journey with a meaningful state transition and one obvious next step.

* Make the same enterprise record accessible through different authorised views, rather than creating a separate record for each portal.

* Make discovery and post-financing monitoring explicit parts of the story.

The most important design principle is this:

The user should experience a simple progression. The underlying platform should preserve the complexity, provenance and continuity.

For NABII, that progression begins with an unattractive paper pack and ends with a structured, discoverable enterprise. For SBGI, it turns observed gaps into targeted TA. For the PFI, it turns discovery into assessment. For the VVB, it turns a specific claim into a governed finding. For the portfolio manager, it turns disconnected updates into a continuously observable portfolio.

And for the SME, the journey doesn't end with one application. It leaves behind a persistent record that can support the next opportunity.

That is the product story I would now use to drive the UI redesign.
